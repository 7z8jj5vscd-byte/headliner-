# Headliner

A music industry career simulator. Start from nothing, write songs, busk, tour the world, and become a legend.

Play it at https://7z8jj5vscd-byte.github.io/headliner-/

To install on iPhone: open the link in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.

## One-Hit Wonder

A second game in this repo, and the reverse of Headliner: you start with a huge viral hit that came out of nowhere. Your relevance drains every month. Labels, managers and hangers-on all want something different from you. Try to avoid becoming a pub-quiz answer and prove you're not a one-hit wonder. Every run gives you a new random hit song, and there are four endings to find.

Play it at https://7z8jj5vscd-byte.github.io/headliner-/one-hit-wonder/

## Debut Dream (K-pop idol simulator)

Another game lives in `kpop/`: create your idol, survive trainee evaluations, debut with a group, chase your first music show win and steer a career all the way to contract renewal and beyond. All agencies, idols and groups are fictional.

Play it at https://7z8jj5vscd-byte.github.io/headliner-/kpop/

Features 3D idols and a dancing group stage (drag to spin), a Dream board of goals, coach-picked weekly plans, a skip-ahead button, a photocard binder and a fan chat. Every release starts with a costume fitting, and each live stage lets you pick a rehearsal method and an ending fairy pose, then plays out as a live broadcast feed with fanchants, chat, mishaps and fancam views. Mini-games come in sets: trainee tests (Rhythm Lanes, Pose Match, Hit the High Note, Echo the Coach, Rhyme Flow), playable rehearsals (Hit Your Marks, Mirror Mirror, Sound Check, Deep Breaths, Find Your Spot, Quick Change), stage games (Camera Catch, Press Q&A), a weekly Practice room, and full Live Stages for showcases, music shows and concerts that chain them section by section. Live stages have synthesized backing tracks, crowd cheers and fanchants, section-by-section choreography with camera cuts and confetti. Releases show hour-by-hour charts and line distribution, and the career adds fansigns, a playable variety show, trending hashtags, a fan cafe, a contract with a dating ban, and dorm life with roommates, chores and member rivalries.

## Decades

Start a band in 1965 and play one year per turn all the way to the present. Manage egos, lineup changes, breakups and reunions while the industry shifts from vinyl to cassettes, music videos, CDs, file-sharing, streaming and viral clips. Your band appears as 3D characters whose clothes and hair follow each decade, a rival band fights you for the charts, and you can replay your whole career as a documentary. Every song plays its own short tune, there are 24 trophies to collect, and the ? button lists every feature and takes you straight to it.

Play it at https://7z8jj5vscd-byte.github.io/headliner-/decades/

## Comeback Tour

A band that was huge twenty-odd years ago split in a blaze of lawsuits and broken mirrors. Now a promoter wants them back, and you, their old manager, have eight weeks to make it happen. Every run generates a new fictional band with a back catalog of hits, a breakup story and five members whose lives went very different ways: one is broke, one is sober, one still hates the others, one has a solo career and one has health problems. Win them back one conversation at a time, settle old grudges, fight over the band name and the publishing split, and hire a replacement the fans may never accept. Then choose a flat guarantee or a versus deal, set ticket prices (dynamic pricing pays and enrages), decide on VIP meet and greets, festival slots, a catalog sale, a biopic and a documentary. Rehearse, lower the keys or don't, decide whether backing tracks go in the system, build the setlist, and take it on the road: every night wears down voices and bodies, plays out song by song and lands with reviews, fan posts and Loopr clips. Feuds can blow up mid-tour, a viral clip can bring in a new generation, and a new album can be a triumph or a shrug. There are six endings: a triumphant legacy, a farewell, a second act, a payday, an implosion and the reunion that never was. The band appears on a 3D stage (drag to spin), ghostly until each member signs.

Play it at https://7z8jj5vscd-byte.github.io/headliner-/comeback/

## A&R: The Talent Scout

You are not the artist. You are the label scout who decides which artists get signed. Every unsigned act has six hidden numbers: talent, work ethic, ego, durability, genre fit and sellability. You never see them, only your read: a range that narrows each time you go to an open mic or a showcase, dig through the Clipstream and Soundpost feeds, listen to their demos (each one plays a short clip), check references, run the streaming data or book a test session. Feeds flatter, and some acts buy their streams. Three rival scouts read the same acts and bid against you. When you make an offer you set the advance, royalty points, recording fund, marketing commitment, 360 share and options, and the game shows the recoupment maths: how many streams before the label breaks even and before the artist sees a royalty. Then the act records, releases and tours, and the truth comes out as gold markers against your read. Hits and flops feed your reputation, your boss's quarterly grades and the climb from Scout to President of A&R, or getting fired. Acts play on a 3D stage, your office fills with plaques (drag to spin), and there is a weekly Pulse 40 chart, trade news, a social feed and an inbox. All artists, labels and scouts are fictional.

Play it at https://7z8jj5vscd-byte.github.io/headliner-/ar/

## Manager: 360 Deal

Run an artist-management company with up to six artists, each with a personality, two career goals, health, morale, trust and jealousy. You get ten blocks of attention a week and a calendar with one lane per artist: book writing weeks, the studio, promo runs, rest and tours, pitch labels, publishers, brands, sync and summer festivals, and decide what to do about burnout, scandals, label delays and drops, band members quitting, commission disputes, rival managers circling and artists feuding with each other. Tours show the real money per show: box office, venue costs, the promoter deal, merch after the venue cut, crew and travel, the agent's 10%, your 15 to 20%, and what the artist actually keeps. Record and publishing deals recoup, majors take a 360, and contracts end with a renewal fight or a sunset clause that keeps paying you for a while. Hire a tour manager, publicist, lawyer, business manager and day-to-day manager, and grow the company's value over five years. Artists perform on a 3D stage sized to the rooms they play, and the office shows your staff and roster (drag to spin). All artists, labels, brands and festivals are fictional.

Play it at https://7z8jj5vscd-byte.github.io/headliner-/manager/

## Festival Tycoon

Found and run your own music festival, one festival year per turn. Play **Story mode** (ten years in two parts, starting on Hollow Farm with your co-founder Jess, Ted the farmer and Victor Crane of rival promoter Monolith Live; bigger sites invite you to move as you grow, and choices change the game and the ending) or **free play** on any of five sites, from a 10,000-capacity farm up to an old airfield for 40,000 or a whole valley for 70,000. In either mode you can move up to a bigger venue once your reputation is high enough. Pick a difficulty (from Chill, the easiest, to Hard) or Sandbox mode with unlimited money, a festival length of two to seven days (always ending on Sunday), and a start year: today, or 1980, 1990, 2000 or 2010 with the real artists of each decade, who come and go as the years pass and an identity: indie mecca, rave weekend, pop party, family festival or eco festival. Book real-world artists, from rising names to global superstars; their fees, popularity and everything that happens to them in the game are made up. The festivals, agencies, brands, press and characters are fictional.

Play it at https://7z8jj5vscd-byte.github.io/headliner-/festival/

Lay out the grounds in a 3D overhead view you can drag, pinch and turn (a site assistant fixes the gaps), with overlays for noise, sightlines, crowd pinch points and wet ground. Build late-night areas, a silent disco, healing and kids' fields, a Discovery stage for unsigned bands and a signature landmark with a Sunday-night ritual; lease the next field as you grow, or launch a spin-off. Book acts through their agents, fight bidding wars with the rival, chase reunion rumours, fix clashes by moving sets, plan secret sets and answer rider requests. Run your festival's own social account to tease the line-up, push tickets and go viral, then watch the general sale open live. During the hour-by-hour weekend, check the weather radar, follow one of your regular fans around the site, grant encores, fire fireworks, confetti or a drone show, and make the calls on storms, curfews and crowd surges. Each year ends with reviews, the Festival Awards, goals with cash bonuses, board meetings, maybe a documentary, a full P&L and a new page in your scrapbook with that year's wristband.

For the long game, every year earns legacy stars that raise your promoter rank and buy perks for future festivals, and every finished festival is graded in a hall of fame. Play eight challenge scenarios for bronze, silver and gold medals, or the daily festival, where everyone gets the same site, artists, weather and twist for the day. Hire crew heads who level up over the years (and get poached), climb the World Festival Rankings, ride each year's world events (genre booms, festival fever, fuel crises, washouts), pick an off-season event every winter, celebrate anniversary editions, and play weekend mini-games: lock the headliner's mix, send stewards to crowd hot spots and fire the finale pyro on the beat. You can share your line-up poster as an image. Pull buzz stunts (billboards, pop-up gigs, celebrity ambassadors, fashion collabs, dance challenges), react to pop-culture moments (a film shoot, a celebrity wedding, a royal visit, the big match, a one-night reunion), and watch more happen on site: big-screen proposals, world-record attempts, surprise collaborations and double rainbows, with a live crowd-and-beat soundtrack during the weekend. Each year pick a fancy-dress theme (neon jungle, space disco, Wild West and more), adopt a festival mascot, let the fans vote for a headliner, gamble on a lucky-dip mystery act, hide golden tickets, chase three weekend quests for bonus cash and choose Saturday night's afterparty.

## Nashville: Make It or Fake It

An unofficial fan game inspired by the TV drama *Nashville*. Make it in Music City as a newcomer you create yourself (choose your look, sound and backstory, from small-town dreamer to Bluebird server), or play as one of the show's characters: Rayna Jaymes, Juliette Barnes, Deacon Claybourne, Scarlett O'Connor, Gunnar Scott, Avery Barkley or Will Lexington. Each one has their own story arc, starting relationships and challenges: Rayna fights the label and her fading relevance, Juliette juggles scandal and her mother Jolene, Deacon guards his sobriety, Scarlett battles stage fright, Will lives a double life.

Every character, from Teddy, Lamar, Maddie, Daphne, Glenn, Bucky and Jeff Fordham to Luke Wheeler, Layla, Zoey, Watty White and Liam, has an illustrated portrait that changes expression. Write songs in a lyric mini-game (alone or co-writing), record them with producers from a session band up to Watty White, release singles to radio with promo budgets and music videos, cut duets and albums that chart and go Gold, and climb a weekly Hot Country Songs chart. Play Lower Broadway, the Bluebird, the Ryman, the Grand Ole Opry, Bridgestone Arena and stadiums in a rhythm game with a synthesized band, or go on tour and open for the stars. Sign with Edgehill, Highway 65 or an indie (with advances to recoup), or start your own label. Date, marry, feud and cheat; survive tabloid scandals with damage control, addiction with AA, sponsors and rehab, and more than 100 events and story beats. Award shows every November, Fan Fest every June, Christmas specials, Opry membership and the Hall of Fame. Show names are used for fan purposes only; the game's songs, lyrics and events are made up.

**Story mode** follows the real show: six seasons of 21, 22, 22, 21, 22 and 16 episodes from October 2012 to 2018, each with three goals, a finale recap with stars and a "previously on" for the next season. The show's biggest moments happen in your world whether or not you're part of them, and your choices change them: the Rayna and Juliette tour, Teddy's election, the viral Bluebird duet, Rayna's crash, Juliette being dropped and joining Highway 65, Luke's proposal, the secret wedding and baby, Deacon's transplant (you can be the donor), the plane crash, Will coming out, the backyard wedding, Jeff Fordham's death, Maddie's emancipation, the Zach Welles buyout, the stalker and Rayna's goodbye (if you play Rayna you can survive it), the family tour and a Ryman tribute finale. Free play keeps the world events without seasons or goals.

If you own a label (Rayna's Highway 65, or your own once you can afford one) a **Label** tab appears: scout and sign the show's characters or new unsigned acts, fund their singles (they chart alongside you), send them on tour, keep their morale up, set a weekly promo budget, hire a label president, and deal with Edgehill poaching your stars, artists' arrests, cash crunches, merger offers and buyout bids. Tours are planned with ticket pricing, production level, merch, VIP meet-and-greets and an opening act, then routed city by city to a Nashville homecoming. The People tab shows **your circle**: fan loyalty (your named fanbase), your label relationship, your manager, your family, your band's morale and your partner, each with things to do about it. Social media has followers, trending hashtags, comments, livestream Q&As, replies to other stars' posts, fan wars, 3 a.m. posts, resurfaced old posts and cancel campaigns.

**3D cast.** Every character is a toon-shaded 3D model built from their look (Rayna's auburn waves, Juliette's platinum hair, Deacon's beard and flannel, Will's straw hat and so on). They act out every event in one of eleven Nashville sets (a listening room, Lower Broadway neon, a label office, a hospital, the red carpet, a press wall, the tour bus highway and more), with mood animations and a TV-style name caption. You also see them on the title-screen stage, your Home screen (drag to spin), character profiles, gig results (performing with a mic or guitar, with surprise guest duets), award nights and the ending. The portraits are kept as a fallback when 3D isn't available.

**Make It or Fake It.** An authenticity score tracks how real you are. Shortcuts (buying streams, a ghostwriter, lip-syncing, a fake romance, bought followers, an invented sob story, lying about sobriety, heavy autotune) give fast results but become secrets. Music Row Insider can expose them, blackmailers can come calling, and a lip-synced show can skip mid-song. Honest moves (unscripted interviews, unplugged sets, teaching, writing your own songs) build it back up, and you can confess everything at once. Authenticity affects awards and decides whether your ending says you made it or faked it. Also new: a wardrobe with a 3D preview, Music Row Insider magazine covers for your biggest weeks, award nights that reveal each category's nominees and winner, rhythm-game sing-offs against rivals, and more events.

**Living Nashville.** Play Bluebird writers' rounds in the circle with Gunnar, Scarlett, Deacon and strangers, picking your song and how you deliver it (stripped, showy, telling the story behind it, or confessing something first), with industry people in the audience. Characters text you between weeks on an in-game phone (exes at 2am, your manager with offers, your mama, a rival being passive-aggressive) and your replies matter. Studio sessions let you pick a producer and band (Deacon on guitar, Brent on fiddle), nail vocal takes in a timing mini-game and add overdubs, and every album gets a cover you design. The world runs without you: stars date, marry, feud, break up and cut duets, and side characters get their own storylines. More moments from the show include the nominations party, the Bluebird anniversary, the Opry's birthday, Juliette's postpartum depression, Teddy's arrest, the custody fight, Scarlett and Gunnar's duo record, Daphne's TV talent show, Deacon's bar, Jessie Caine and Cash Gray. ## Dream Academy: Story Mode

An unofficial fan game that follows the shape of the real story behind the global girl group from The Debut: Dream Academy: the 2021 open call, a year of training, the 2023 survival show, the debut and the first years as a group, then twelve years in all (144 months). It uses the twenty real contestants' names and stylized looks you can edit. Every song, era, chart, eval, skill number, quote and bit of drama is invented. It is not affiliated with HYBE, Geffen, Netflix or anyone in it.

Play it at https://7z8jj5vscd-byte.github.io/headliner-/dreamacademy/

**How it plays.** One turn is one month. Pick a focus member, then plan each member's month by hand or put her on autoplay (Balanced, Skill Grind, Fan Service or Protect Wellbeing), and switch between them at any time. Monthly evals grade everyone from D to A+ during training, and the bottom three get a warning. The survival show runs three missions and a live finale. Choose Real result to follow the real eliminations and final six (with the real finale scores), or Open result to let scores and votes decide. Key stages are a short tap-timing game with a skip button.

**Drama.** Tensions arise between members and contestants in survival-show style: team picks, the center spot, practice clashes, rivalries, misunderstandings, line distribution, concept fights, two camps, spotlight, label favoritism, push-or-rest and writing credits. Each has heat that rises every month nobody deals with it, a bond score between the two, confessional quotes, and four ways to handle it. The Drama tab shows a bond web. Contestants who were minors on the show and contestants who did not debut only get light, competitive drama. Nothing invents bullying, betrayal, crimes or scandals.

**After the debut.** A group schedule (training, recording, promotion, touring, recovery), releases with concepts and invented titles, a fictional chart, viral dance moments, a brand campaign, awards nights, a festival with a surprise guest, a hometown arena show, label pressure, hate trains aimed at the group (never one member by name) with statement, silence, standing together or apology, breaks of any length that never end a run, contract renewals and five endings.

**Saves.** Autosave on the device, plus export and import of a save code.

## Updates go live automatically

Any push to a `claude/...` branch is merged into `main` by `.github/workflows/auto-publish.yml`, and GitHub Pages publishes `main`. No pull request is needed. If an automatic merge ever conflicts, GitHub emails the repo owner and that branch can be merged with a normal pull request instead.

## The Eras Life

A week-by-week career game through Taylor Swift's documented public career, from the first demo tapes in 2001 to the end of 2026. After that, the story is yours to carry on with invented songs, eras and drama. It is an unofficial fan project: it uses no lyrics, audio, album art or photos, and event cards stick to the public record, with no invented private dialogue or quotes.

Play it at https://7z8jj5vscd-byte.github.io/headliner-/eras/

**How it plays.** Each week you get Time Blocks (6, 5 or 4 depending on difficulty) to spend on required and optional tasks. Working past your blocks costs Energy. Every album moves through stages: Writing, Recording, Finishing, Pre-release, Release, Promotion, Tour prep, On tour and Rest. Stages overlap, so you might be writing Speak Now while touring Fearless. Real release dates never move: if you fall behind, the album still comes out on time, but with lower quality and weaker first-week numbers. Real events interrupt the week with a decision. Your choice changes your stats and strategy, but never whether the event happened. Each card ends with "What really happened", and your Canon Score measures how close you stayed to her real choices. Sensitive events are told briefly and factually, and you can respond only with support, safety or a statement. Fast-forward skips quiet weeks but stops at every real event and deadline.

**Real-world events.** World events fire on their real dates in every save, in both Story Mode and Era Start: September 11, the iTunes Store, YouTube, the iPhone, the 2008 financial crisis, Spotify's US launch, streams counting on the charts, the Manchester and Las Vegas attacks, TikTok, COVID-19, Blackout Tuesday, vaccines, live music's return, inflation, the Hollywood strikes and the 2025 Los Angeles fires. Some change what you can do. The recession lowers ticket demand and sponsor fees, and security and inflation raise touring costs. During the COVID-19 lockdown (March 2020 to mid-2021) there are no concerts or fan events: interviews go to video, fan events become livestreams, release week goes remote, and any tour that falls in the lockdown is cancelled and its tickets refunded. You can still write, often better, and record at home.

**Lover Fest and the pandemic.** Announce Lover Fest in September 2019 and plan it in the Tour Desk as a short run of stadium festival dates. Tickets go on sale. Then the pandemic cancels it in April 2020: every ticket is refunded and the production deposit is lost, as really happened. Her other pandemic-era moments are cards too: the City of Lover concert and its broadcast in lockdown, folklore made remotely, the Long Pond Studio Sessions, and the Grammys moved to March.

**Out of public view (2016 to 2017).** After the 2016 fallout you can step out of sight. Public tasks (posts, interviews, fan events, publicist shoots) go away, but you can keep writing and recording Reputation, hold secret writing sessions, write songs for other artists under another name, quietly read fan theories and meet your team. Press Heat cools fast. You come back with the August 2017 social media wipe, or earlier from the Press tab.

**Modes.** Story Mode starts with a short prologue and plays every week from 2001 on. Era Start drops you into any era Story Mode has reached, with that moment's catalog and team. The event track is off, and you can move release dates. From January 2027 the real track ends and free play begins, with a Drama Engine of invented leaks, backlash, rival pop stars and tabloids. It never invents misconduct or personal crises for real people.

**The 13 tabs**, each unlocking when it would in her real career: This Week, Life, Songbook (only the current era's real songs; vault songs stay locked until their Taylor's Version), Studio (producers, editions, re-recording), Rollout (singles, videos, sponsors, approvals), Cover Studio (an original cover for every album from palettes, abstract images, layouts and finishes, or your own image, which stays on your device), Tour Desk (venues, nights, prices, staging, openers and setlist, approved or sent back by your manager and publicist, and measured against each real tour's gross), Team Inbox, Easter Eggs (plant clues, watch invented fan accounts decode them, and collect the real eggs in an Egg Log), Fandom, Press and People, Charts and Awards (Beyoncé and Lady Gaga appear as chart rivals on their real release dates), and Legacy (era gallery, goals, achievements, ownership and your timeline beside hers).

**3D and era looks.** The performer is a stylised 3D toon caricature whose look changes with every era: the hair (curls, then straight with bangs, the bob, braids), the make-up (red lips from Red on) and the colour and silhouette of each era's stage costumes. You see it as a child in the early years, in the Eras Tour's changing acts, and in casual clothes for writing and studio weeks. It stands on a 3D set that follows your stage, and Beyoncé and Lady Gaga appear in their own period looks on the cards they are part of and in their story previews. Drag to spin.

**Success that follows the real eras.** Album first weeks, fan growth, Hot 100 peaks for the well-known singles, and tour grosses are anchored to how each era really went. A momentum score (shown in Fandom) scales them: play better than she did and you beat the real numbers, play worse and you fall short. The fan curve is a rough game-scale estimate, and the other figures are approximate, so they still need the fact-check.

**Saves.** Three slots, autosave every week, and export and import of save files.

Beyoncé's and Lady Gaga's stories are mapped and can be previewed from the title screen. They become playable once Taylor's story has proved the systems. Dates and figures come from the design report and still need a card-by-card fact-check.
